About

Nate Palmer is Lecturer in International Environmental Law at SOAS University of London, where he is Lead on Experiential Learning and Engagement at the Law, Environment and Development Centre and co-coordinates the SOAS Environmental Justice Clinic. His research examines Rights of Nature, water governance and environmental justice, combining doctrinal and socio-legal methods. His doctoral thesis, completed at SOAS in 2026 with a Bloomsbury PhD Studentship, developed a framework for water governance grounded in harmony with Nature, drawing on fieldwork in Delhi and Meghalaya. He is an Expert Member of the UN Harmony with Nature Programme and Assistant Editor of the Law, Environment and Development (LEAD) Journal.

Research and publications

  1. 2026

    Harmony with Nature: Evolving Dimensions of Water Governance in India

    PhD thesisSOAS University of London

    This thesis develops a non-anthropocentric framework for water governance grounded in the principles of harmony with Nature and offers a pathway toward more equitable, ecologically responsive, and legally coherent approaches to water governance.

    Argument in brief. Dominant water governance models rest on instrumental, human-centred assumptions that sideline plural understandings of water. Through a case study of India, focused on Meghalaya, the thesis shows how these models are reproduced across local, national and international scales, and argues that just water governance must rebalance the intrinsic, relational and instrumental values of water.

    Nate Palmer, ‘Harmony with Nature: Evolving Dimensions of Water Governance in India’ (PhD thesis, SOAS University of London 2026) https://doi.org/10.25501/soas.1122765

  4. Forthcoming

    Who Speaks for Nature? Rights of Nature, Biocultural Rights, and Pluricentric Legal Design

    AuthorJournal article, publication pending

    Proposes a framework of pluricentric legal design that grounds legal obligation in the relationship between communities and ecosystems.

    Argument in brief. Rights of Nature and biocultural rights both answer the question of who speaks for Nature by deriving obligation from legal subjecthood, whether ecological or human. The article argues for grounding obligation in the relationship between community and ecosystem instead, with ecological integrity as a legal priority and guardianship as accountable institutional voice.

Clinic projects

2025–26

A Charter for the River Thames

Co-supervisorLSE Sustainability Law and Policy Clinic, intercollegiate, with the Environmental Law Foundation

Three reports, April 2026, testing legal routes to a Thames Charter that treats the river as a stakeholder in decisions affecting it. The project was London's first intercollegiate environmental law clinic, with LSE, King's, UCL, SOAS and Birkbeck. Its strands looked at a joint borough arrangement under the Local Government Act 1972, at byelaw powers, and at bringing the Thames into decision-making procedures.

I co-supervised the decision-making strand with Dr Marie Petersmann. Its team compared three routes for giving the Thames a voice within the Greater London Authority: representation through the Mayoral team, an advisory role alongside the London Assembly Environment Committee, and integration into existing strategies such as the London Plan. They concluded that strategy-based integration is likely the most feasible first step.

Strand team: Josie Butcher, Sarah Cooper, Julia Peetz, Hazal Pekdemir, Catalina Racovita and Kinjell Singh.

2025/26

Practitioners’ Guide: The Potential for Nature (Land) “Owning Itself” in the United Kingdom

Co-supervisorSOAS Clinic with Lawyers for Nature

A practitioners’ guide on whether land could “own itself” in the UK. The team assessed comparative models and English land law and concluded that land cannot literally own itself, because it lacks legal personality, but that charitable trusts, conservation covenants and purpose-driven stewardship structures may approximate some of its aims.

Team: Lina Zeitoun, Beatrice Stuart Fisher, Flavia Russo, Tom Crumbie and Jeffrey Cheung. Co-supervised with Miguel Camilo Cuba Pinto.
